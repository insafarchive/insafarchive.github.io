## 1. Executive Summary

This audit examines every legal citation, superior court judgment, interim order, and appellate directive referenced across the Insaf Archive dossiers.

A critical failure in human rights and current affairs documentation is the conflation of:
1. An **interim stay order** with a **final judgment**
2. An **allegation recorded by a judge** during hearing with a **judicial finding of fact**
3. A **short order** whose operation has been subsequently **suspended on appeal** with settled binding law
4. **Obiter dicta / observations** with the **ratio decidendi / binding holding**

In this audit, every cited legal instrument was inspected for its exact procedural status and holding.

### Citation 1: Civilian Military Courts Short Order (Const. Pet. 24/2023)
- **Citation ID:** `cit-sc-mil-courts-2023`
- **Dossier:** Military Court Trials of Civilians (`dos-pk-legal-military-courts-2023`)
- **Case Title:** *Jawwad S. Khawaja & Others v Federation of Pakistan*
- **Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan (Original Constitutional Jurisdiction under Art. 184(3))
- **Case Number:** Constitution Petition No. 24 of 2023 (alongside Const. Pet. Nos. 25, 26, 27, 28, 30 of 2023)
- **Judgment Date:** 23 October 2023
- **Report Citation:** Unreported Short Order (Detailed reasons pending from several bench members)
- **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 1, 2, 3, and 4 of Operative Short Order
- **Decision Type:** `majority_opinion` (4–1 on Section 2(1)(d); Justice Yahya Afridi dissenting in part)
- **Procedural Posture:** **STAYED ON APPEAL.** On 13 December 2023, a 6-judge larger bench in Intra-Court Appeal No. 558 of 2023 stayed the operation of this short order by a 5–1 majority.
- **Actual Holding Cited in Dossier:** Section 2(1)(d)(i) & (ii) and Section 59(4) of the Pakistan Army Act 1952 declared ultra vires the Constitution. Detainees ordered to be tried by ordinary criminal courts.
- **Verification Verdict:** `stayed_on_appeal` / `requires_legal_review`
- **Audit Finding:** The dossier must not present this short order as an operative settled constitutional judgment. The dossier has been **downgraded to `under_review`** and explicitly updated to document the 13 December 2023 larger bench stay order.

### Citation 3: National Assembly Dissolution Landmark Judgment (PLD 2022 SC 574)
- **Citation ID:** `cit-sc-na-dissolution-2022`
- **Dossier:** National Assembly Dissolution & Restoration (`dos-pk-legal-assembly-dissolution-2022`)
- **Case Title:** *Suo Motu Case No. 1 of 2022 (Regarding Dismissal of No-Confidence Resolution and Dissolution of National Assembly)*
- **Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan
- **Case Number:** Suo Motu Case No. 1 of 2022 (heard alongside Const. Pet. Nos. 10 to 14 of 2022)
- **Judgment Date:** 7 April 2022 (Short Order) / Detailed Reasons authored by CJP Umar Ata Bandial
- **Report Citation:** **PLD 2022 SC 574**
- **Relevant Paragraphs:** Paragraphs 12–16 (Article 58(1) Explanation analysis) & Paragraphs 22–25 (Article 69 non-applicability to constitutional ultra vires)
- **Decision Type:** `final_judgment` / `binding_holding` (Unanimous 5–0 Larger Bench)
- **Procedural Posture:** **FINAL & BINDING PRECEDENT.** No review petition was admitted to alter this holding.
- **Actual Holding Cited in Dossier:** The Prime Minister's power to advise dissolution under Article 58(1) is extinguished the moment a notice of a resolution for a vote of no-confidence is tabled under Article 95; Deputy Speaker's dismissal of the vote without division was contrary to the Constitution and void ab initio; National Assembly stood restored immediately.
- **Verification Verdict:** `verified_holding`
- **Audit Finding:** Textual alignment between the holding cited and PLD 2022 SC 574 is exact. Ready for publication.
